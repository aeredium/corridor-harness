## Spec T10 — Every person is brought in on their own credential, and the harness proves the quorum (20 September 2026)

From the fourth live run against Harness Holdings (report `aer360-harness-2026-09-20-135419.md`, station S6): Ben's press after Ada's was counted as Ada's, because the two shared one credential. AER 360 Spec 91 (aeredium/AERAccounts, commit 9964205, PR #112) then made an `author` invitation mint a credential of the person's own on the account's role-bearing policy entry, bound to the passkey on redemption; it left a passkey enrolled before it speaking for the shared credential until that credential is revoked at the platform, and a seat bound to the shared credential bound until it is re-granted. The harness's three people were enrolled before Spec 91, and Ada's seat was granted before it.

- `aer360_harness.py`, S4: reads Ada's seat as the run finds it; after each person signs in with their stored passkey, compares the session's `credentialId` with the founder's. Equal is the shared credential of S10's finding: S4 says so and brings the person in again — the founder mints a new `author` invitation for the same name and email (`mint_invite`), the person redeems it with a NEW software passkey (`enrol_by_invite`) stored beside the old one as `<person>-2-<date>.json` and never over it (`next_key_path`), and the session is the new one. The step's expected line is *a credential of <name>'s own, not the founder's*; the result names the last four characters of both. Different is *already on their own credential*, and nothing is minted. A fresh enrolment that lands on the shared credential (an estate before Spec 91) is reported and not re-invited in the same run. A refusal anywhere on the road is told in the estate's words, and the person keeps the session and the passkey they had.
- `aer360_harness.py`, S4: Ada's seat is granted again (`POST /v1/approver-seats/grant`) where it is `enrolled_not_seated` or names a credential other than her session's; the line says which credential the seat named as found, after the people were brought in, and after the grant; a grant that does not name her own credential, or one the estate refuses, is a finding in the estate's words, and S4 fails.
- `aer360_harness.py`, the store: `load_passkeys` signs each person in with the newest passkey stored for them at this base and leaves older ones alone; `save_passkey` writes a passkey back to the file it came from (`Person.key_file`) and never to another key's; `stored_key_paths`, `next_key_path`, `retired_passkeys`. `aer360_passkey.py`: `stored_key_paths` and `next_key_path` name a person's key files, oldest first, `<stem>-<n>-<date>.json` beside `<stem>.json`.
- `aer360_harness.py`, `audit_people` (S10): asserts, after S4, that the sessions carry distinct credential ids and none but the founder's is the founder's, and says so as a note — so the closing table's last-run column says *closed* for *one credential for several people*; the finding, where it stands, names the founder's credential. Reads the register's Spec 91 marker (`sharesCredentialWith`, spoken as the People screen speaks it: `shares_credential_sentence`, from `packages/shared/src/enrolment.ts`) for each of the harness's people: expected absent after the re-invitation, a finding where it stands, quoting the register and the screen's sentence. The founder's row is the one marker the harness expects: the passkeys it retired still speak for the founder's credential at the estate, which does nothing to them by itself, so a marker naming exactly their labels is a note; any other name is a finding. A register without the field (an estate before Spec 91) is a note.
- `aer360_harness.py`, `--dry` and the frozen fixture: under S4, the seats as the run finds them, then for each author the comparison and the conditional re-invitation (invites, invite/options, invite/verify with a NEW passkey stored beside the old), the seat re-grant's condition, and the register read; `tests/fixtures/aer360-dry-calls.txt` is updated for these lines and nothing else (121 → 134 lines).
- Tests: the double learns Spec 91 (`tests/test_aer360_double.py`): an author invitation mints a credential of the person's own on the one policy entry bearing authorship (several are `INVITE_INVALID`, listing entries and credentials); redemption binds the passkey and retires the bindings an earlier invitation of the same address enrolled (`person.credential_replaced`); `GET /v1/invites` marks each person's newest redeemed row with the other labels whose passkeys speak for its credential; a shared credential is refused a seat by name (`APPROVER_SEAT_CREDENTIAL_SHARED`, 409, the sentence composed as `approverSeatSharedSentence` composes it) and is never seated by the redemption road; several credentials on one address are `APPROVER_SEAT_AMBIGUOUS` (409); the change governance is established once and a recompile leaves the rosters standing (`governanceAlreadyStood`); and a ceremony press is counted by the platform's rule, established by Spec 91's builder from `validateMultisigSigner` — matched by credential first, an empty seat bound by the address the estate's press names, a seat bound to another key refused *not authorized* — which corrects Spec T9's own choice of matching by email. Knobs: `before_spec_91` (the estate of the four live runs), `seat_completes_on_redemption=False` (before Spec 58's count 3), `second_authorship_entry`, `platform_names_approver` (an operator at the platform's console naming a minted credential a second approver). Each of the spec's tests was red on main: against the pre-91 shape with four people on one credential, S4 re-invites three, stores three new passkeys with the suffix, the seat names Ada's new credential (seated on redemption; the grant road proven where the redemption seats nobody), the sessions carry four distinct credentials and S10 raises no credential finding, closing the old one in the last-run column; where the credentials already differ nothing is minted and each person keeps their one passkey; a seat grant refused is a finding with the estate's sentence; the old passkey files differ after the re-invitation only in the counter the one sign-in moved, and a run after it leaves them byte for byte; `--dry` carries the conditional lines.

The answers to the interviews are fenced, so the harness cannot seat Ben; the cure is the estate's (a guard that admits the roster's members, and a seat rebound when a credential is replaced) and is Bear's to decide.

## Spec T9 — A payee is whitelisted when its quorum is met, and the harness presses for the quorum (20 September 2026)

From the estate harness's second live run against Harness Holdings (report `aer360-harness-2026-09-20-103245.md`, station S6), the estate's audit trail and the compiler's own roster. S6 created two payees, promoted each and had Ada approve once, then expected `whitelisted`; both stayed `pending_promotion`, and the audit row said why — `requiredSignatures 2, signaturesCollected 1`. The charter Harness Holdings confirmed answered C12 = 2 and C12A = No, so the `whitelist_mutation` roster's threshold is two and its members are the census of A8 (`governanceSignersFor`, `onboardingcompiler.ts`): Harriet Founder, Ada Approver, Ben Signatory, Cora Clerk. Four of two is a roster that can meet its quorum; the harness had pressed once and called the estate's honest `pending_promotion` a failure. Read from aeredium/AERAccounts at commit 7d809e1 (Spec 89), `routes/payees.ts`, `services/payees.ts` and `services/onboardingcompiler.ts`.

- `aer360_harness.py`, S6: presses until the quorum is met. After Ada's approval, while the answer's `whitelistStatus` is `pending_promotion` and `approvals.remaining` is above zero, the next roster member approves — the first name in `may_still_approve` for whom the harness holds a signed-in session (excluding the founder, who shares the one credential; a distinct author's press is what moves the count), else Ben Signatory, else the founder. Each press is a step with the presser's name; the loop stops at `whitelisted`, at a refusal, or after `approvals.required` presses, and never presses more times than `WHITELIST_ROSTER` has names. Where the estate answers without `approvals` (before Spec 89), one further press is made as Ben on the charter's quorum of two, with a note. The station's line names each payee's presses in order: *Northwind Supplies: created; promoted; approved by Ada Approver (1 of 2), by Ben Signatory (2 of 2): whitelisted*, and the register's status is read from the row whose `id` is this run's `payee_id`, never by address (every run creates its payees afresh, and earlier runs leave rows with the same addresses still pending).
- `aer360_harness.py`, S6: the first approval's answer is audited (findings of S10's kind, raised in S6, carrying the answer verbatim): *a pending approval that did not say why (Spec 89)* where it lacks `approvals`, `may_still_approve` or `sentence`; *a quorum that exceeds its roster* where `approvals.required` exceeds the names in `may_still_approve` plus `approvals.collected`; and a sentence that does not begin `"<collected> of <required> approvals recorded for this address."`.
- `aer360_answers.py`: `WHITELIST_ROSTER` (the census people, from `CENSUS_ORDER`) and `WHITELIST_QUORUM` (the figure answered at C12), with the compiler's line named, so the harness's expectation is the charter's; a test proves the two agree with the answers given at A8 and C12.
- `aer360_harness.py`, `--dry` and the frozen fixture: S6 carries the second press, *(as the next roster member the first answer names, expected Ben Signatory) → expect whitelistStatus whitelisted*; `tests/fixtures/aer360-dry-calls.txt` is updated for these lines and nothing else (119 → 121 lines).
- Tests: the double's approve answers as Spec 89's estate does — the first press `pending_promotion` with `approvals {required 2, collected 1, remaining 1}`, `may_still_approve` and the sentence, composed word for word; the press by a different roster member `whitelisted`; a press by a key on no seat relayed as `SIGNATURE_NOT_COUNTED` with the platform's words. Knobs: `pending_approval_says_why=False` (the estate before Spec 89), `whitelist_roster` (a roster below its quorum), `platform_never_activates` (a platform that counts every signature and never activates). Each of the spec's tests was red on main.

From the first live run (report `aer360-harness-2026-09-20.md`) and Bear's rulings of the same morning: three lines of that run said "fail" where the estate was right or where nothing was wrong. - `aer360_harness.py`, S11: the venue probe expects acceptance. "Unless it is stipulated explicitly in the questionnaire, it must be accepted" (Bear, 20 September 2026): the questionnaire decides policy, and a contract is an address. HTTP 201 is reported as *accepted, as the law says*, and a refusal is the finding, with the ruling quoted beside the estate's sentence; the probe's docstring carries the ruling and the day the expectation flips.
- `aer360_harness.py`, S10: the read-back is compared by content. `spoken_for` renders in the estate's own forms — `services/onboarding.ts`, the `spoken` switch, read from the code: a list entry's values in the order the estate stores them, a currency as its code, money as the dollars-and-cents sentence, a roster as the names joined by ", " — and returns None for a kind it cannot render, which S10 records as *not compared: no rendering for kind <k>*, a note and never a finding. - `aer360_harness.py`, the report: when a previous report stands in the working folder, the closing table gains a *last run* column — the station's previous outcome and each finding's fate, closed, still open or new, matched by station and probe with the ids a run mints set aside — and the findings are listed by fate below the count. `read_report`, `find_last_report`, `finding_key` and `compare_findings` read a report this harness wrote, in Spec T7's shape or this one's. ## Spec T7 — The estate harness (20 September 2026)

- New: `aer360_harness.py` — the runner: the invitation and sign-in ceremonies, the interviews answered from the book, the people, the wallet account, the payees, the payments, the journey; the three hats (S10 the auditor, S11 the attacker, S12 the optimizer); the dry run; the report.
